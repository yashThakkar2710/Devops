One architecture, grown over four weeks

Not thirty disconnected tutorials. The same system gets better each week, so by day 30 you have built the architecture the exam describes.

Week 1

A server, inside a network you built by hand.

Week 2

It survives an availability zone failure and scales under load.

Week 3

It becomes event-driven, encrypted and locked down.

Week 4

It gets monitored, cost-optimised and given a recovery plan.

How each day runs

The same five steps every time. Step three is where the understanding actually forms.

Concept first

What problem does this service solve? 20–30 minutes, before any clicking.

Build it

Console first to understand it, CLI second to repeat it. Most of the time goes here.

Break it

Remove a permission, kill an instance, block a port. Read the error. Anyone can follow a tutorial; reading the failure is the skill.

Tear it down

Same day, without exception.

Record it

Notes to docs/, commands to reference/cheatsheets/, questions to recall/.

On videos

Welcome as a second explanation, after you have built the thing. Not before — video makes you feel you understand something you have not done, and that feeling is hard to tell apart from the real thing until an exam question asks you to choose.
